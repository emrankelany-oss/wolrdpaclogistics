## Why Jounieh Now? The Case for Visiting in 2025


Jounieh isn't the Lebanon you saw in the news last year. The October 2024 conflict has ended, and this coastal city of roughly **96,000 to 100,000 residents** is moving forward with unmistakable momentum. Beirut might grab headlines, but Jounieh—just 25 kilometers north—offers something bolder: an emerging destination backed by real infrastructure investment.


> **"Jounieh represents Lebanon's willingness to rebuild,"** observes local development officials. The Jounieh Téléphérique cable car system recovered from its 2024 incident to carry over **300,000 passengers annually** again. That's evidence of a city refusing to pause.


The fundamentals tell the story. Jounieh is **97.71% Christian**, with Maronite Catholics comprising **71.19%** of registered voters. This demographic stability creates a different rhythm than Beirut—calmer, more cohesive, less volatile. Meanwhile, the infrastructure narrative is real: a $40 million touristic port project is in motion, Cyprus ferry routes are planned, and the EU is paying attention.






## The Jounieh Téléphérique: 60 Years of Ascent


The red cable car cutting up Jounieh's mountainside greets every visitor. Since 1965, the Jounieh Téléphérique has transported **over 14 million passengers**. It's not just mechanics—it's lived history for Lebanese families who've ridden it across generations.


                ![Iconic red Jounieh Téléphérique cable car ascending steep, lush green mountain with city and Mediterranean bay visible below](images/slot-1-contextual-f78920ad.jpeg)
                The iconic Jounieh Téléphérique: 60 years of connecting city to mountain.



The mechanics are clean: a **1.5-kilometer cable** hoists you 650 meters in exactly **9 minutes**. Pre-crisis, it carried **400,000 passengers annually**. The 2024 cable-snapping incident threatened that narrative. Recovery was swift instead. Passenger numbers rebounded to **300,000+**. That rebound signals something: Jounieh doesn't quit.


                🎯 Key Insight

Cable car recovery signals investor confidence. When locals and tourists rush to ride it again, infrastructure operators notice. That momentum fuels the larger port investment coming 2025-2026.

## A Day in Jounieh: The 6-Step Itinerary


One day works. Here's how to pack in the essentials without burnout.



                1.
### Morning: Ride the Téléphérique (8:30–10:00 AM)


Get there early. Weekend slots fill fast. Ascend in 9 minutes, arrive at Harissa. Visit the Our Lady of Lebanon statue and grotto shrine. Early birds reward themselves: no crowds, clear light, Mediterranean sprawling beneath.

                2.
### Late Morning: Harissa Basilica (10:00–11:30 AM)


The Maronite pilgrimage site draws year-round crowds. Sacred or secular, the architecture arrests you. Grab cardamom coffee nearby—thick, sweet, unapologetic.

                3.
### Lunch: Bkerké or Sahel Alma (12:00–1:30 PM)


Bkerké is the Maronite Patriarchate seat—less tourist trap, more living faith center. Sahel Alma village nearby has better restaurants. Try grilled fish and fresh lemon juice. Budget: $10–15 per person.

                4.
### Afternoon: Old Stone Souk (2:30–4:30 PM)


Jounieh's cultural heartbeat. Restored sandstone alleyways, antique shops, cafes tucked everywhere. No tourist traps—locals actually shop here. Sit, order mint tea ($2), let the afternoon drift.

                5.
### Late Afternoon: Waterfront Walk (4:30–6:00 PM)


The Bay waterfront is rebuilt. Walk the promenade, check new marina infrastructure. The sunset photographs beautifully. Skip swimming—we'll address bay water quality next.

                6.
### Evening: Casino du Liban or Dinner (7:00 PM+)


Nightlife appeal? Casino du Liban is Jounieh's social hub. Formal dress required. Or skip it: order fresh seafood harborside. Budget $25–50 per person. Credit cards increasingly common; cash still reigns elsewhere.








## The Environmental Reality: Swimming, Pollution, and Responsible Tourism


Jounieh Bay is stunning. It's also contaminated. Skip the city? No. Visit responsibly? Absolutely.



                  ⚠️ Concerns


                    - Marine sediment Aluminum: 1,170.31 ppm (high)

                    - Chromium levels: 90.9 ppm (coastal high)

                    - Industrial/port activity linked

                    - Meso-oligotrophic bay system





                  ✓ Alternatives


                    - Skip bay swimming entirely

                    - Use Jounieh Beach clubs (filtered pools)

                    - Visit nearby Bouma Beach (cleaner)

                    - Support local ReYACt cleanup efforts







**The reality:** Heavy metal contamination—Aluminum at 1,170 ppm, Chromium at 91 ppm—is documented. Local groups like ReYACt Forum are organizing cleanups, pushing municipal action. Visit responsibly: enjoy the view, skip the bay swim, use filtered beach club pools, eat from licensed restaurants (they source carefully).


                **Is Jounieh Bay safe for swimming?**

No. Skip it. Heavy metal contamination from decades of port and industrial activity makes bay swimming unsafe. Use chlorinated beach club pools instead.




                **Can I eat Jounieh seafood safely?**

Yes, with care. Licensed restaurants source from certified suppliers who avoid contaminated zones. Stick to established places. Properly sourced and prepared seafood is safe.




                **What's being done about bay pollution?**

The Council for Development and Reconstruction (CDR) has environmental assessments underway. The new port project (Grand Jounieh, 2025–2026) includes Phase 1 dredging and sediment removal priorities. Local cleanup initiatives run regularly.

### Harissa / Our Lady of Lebanon


The iconic statue and pilgrimage basilica. Year-round Maronite Catholic visits. Secular travelers appreciate the architecture and views equally. 9 minutes on the cable car to reach.





### Casino du Liban


Jounieh's nightlife epicenter. Gaming, restaurants, live entertainment. Formal dress required. Books international acts summer. Peak Thursday–Saturday after 11 PM.





### Old Stone Souk


Restored historical marketplace. Artisan shops, antiques, galleries, afternoon cafes. No tourist traps. Locals actually browse here. Admission: free to walk.





### Bkerké (Maronite Patriarchate)


The spiritual Lebanese Maronite center. Less tourist spot, more living faith institution. Visitors welcome during non-service hours. Stunning grounds.





### Jounieh Bay Waterfront


Promenade walk, new marina infrastructure, sunset views. Skip swimming. Perfect for evening walks and waterfront dining.





### Sahel Alma


Village adjacent to Bkerké. Mountain village with superior food options. Traditional Lebanese homes, small shops. Excellent for lunch if you skip the Patriarchate.








## Frequently Asked Questions




                    Is Jounieh safe to visit in 2025?
                    ▼

                  Yes. The October 2024 conflict ended. Jounieh is calmer than Beirut—smaller, more stable population. Standard precautions apply: avoid politics, don't photograph military installations, stay aware of surroundings. Tourism infrastructure is operational.




                    How do I get to Jounieh from Beirut?
                    ▼

                  Drive (25 km, ~45 minutes via coastal highway) or taxi. Uber/Careem work both cities. No direct trains. Car rental is recommended if exploring beyond downtown. Budget $30–50 for a taxi day rate.




                    How much will I spend in a day?
                    ▼

                  Budget $60–120 per person: $10–15 cable car, $20–30 meals (3 meals), $10–15 attractions, $15–30 transport/activities. Staying overnight? Add $214 avg. for Airbnb or $80–150 budget hotel.




                    Should I book accommodations in advance?
                    ▼

                  Not urgent—Jounieh has low occupancy (26.9% average). Book 2–3 weeks ahead for peak season (June–August). Walk-ins can often negotiate better off-season rates. Airbnb is reliable; souk boutique hotels are also good.




                    When is the best time to visit?
                    ▼

                  April–June (spring, perfect weather, few crowds). September–October (early autumn, warm). Avoid July–August (hot, crowded). Winter (December–February) is mild but rainy. Cable car runs year-round.




                    Can I visit on a day trip from Beirut?
                    ▼

                  Yes. Follow Section 5's itinerary for one packed day. Leave Beirut at 8 AM, return by 8 PM. Hit the cable car, Harissa, the souk, the waterfront. Not rushed if you skip the casino and skip extra meals.




                    Is Jounieh walkable?
                    ▼

                  Partially. The souk and waterfront are pedestrian-friendly. Harissa is cable-car accessible. Bkerké and Sahel Alma need transport. Wear comfortable shoes—souk alleys are steep.




                    What's changing in 2025–2026?
                    ▼

                  Grand Jounieh port (Phase 1, $8M) is under construction. Cyprus ferry routes finalize. New hotels and restaurants arrive. Visit now for the "before," or return in 2026 for the after.
